# Casgevy and sickle cell disease

The US Food and Drug Administration approved Casgevy on 8 December 2023 for people aged 12 and older with sickle cell disease who have recurrent vaso-occlusive crises [1]. It was the first approved medicine to use CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing [1].

## What the treatment does

Casgevy is made from a patient's own blood stem cells. The cells are collected, edited to switch a gene called BCL11A down, and returned by infusion [2]. With that gene turned down, red blood cells make more fetal haemoglobin, which does not sickle [2].

## What the trial found

In the main trial, 29 of 31 patients followed for long enough had no severe pain crises for at least 12 consecutive months after treatment [1]. None of those 31 patients had a graft fail or be rejected [1].

## Limits of the evidence

The trial was small and had no comparison group, so the size of the benefit against standard care is not measured directly [3]. Patients must first have high-dose chemotherapy to clear their bone marrow, which carries a risk of infertility and infection [2]. Follow-up so far is a few years, and the regulator has asked for a 15-year study of long-term safety [1].
